# Product Requirements Document: Handmade Accessories Store

Oct 12, 2026 · @anvi

## 1. Problem statement

Fabric accessories are personal: people care which fabric, size and finish they get, but most stores sell fixed items, and custom orders happen over WhatsApp and Instagram messages. A client, Chic Wardrobe, is launching a handmade accessories brand and needs a website where customers can browse, personalise and buy without messaging back and forth.

**The problem today:**

- Customisation is handled manually through chat, which is slow and hard to track.
- Customers can't see their choices, the price, or the finished look before ordering.
- Orders, addresses and payments have no single place to live.

**Why it matters:** a clear, beautiful ordering experience makes the brand look established, reduces back-and-forth for the owners, and lets customers buy at any hour.

## 2. Target users

The audience details below are working assumptions drawn from the product range and references. The client should confirm them.

| User | Who they are | What they want | What frustrates them |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personaliser | Someone buying a scrunchie, pouch or sleeve for themselves and wanting a specific fabric and size | To pick fabric, size and details and see the result and price before paying | Not knowing what the finished item looks like or what it will cost |
| Gift buyer | Someone buying for a friend or family member | A quick, attractive way to choose, with clear delivery and order status | Long chats to place an order, unclear delivery timing |
| Returning customer | Someone who has ordered before | To find past orders, reuse a saved address and reorder | Re-entering details every time |

**Technical comfort:** assume comfortable with mobile apps and online payments. The site must work well on phones, since most visitors are likely to arrive from Instagram or WhatsApp links (to be confirmed with the client).

## 3. Product vision

A premium, independent-feeling handmade brand online, where choosing your own fabric and details is as enjoyable as receiving the product. Real photography and fabric texture lead the design, and the customisation experience is the centrepiece.

## 5. App flow

The customer journey runs from landing to order tracking. Every step must show clear loading, empty, error and success states.

1. **Land on the homepage.** The visitor sees featured products, categories and the brand story, with a clear path to Shop All and Build Your Own.
2. **Create an account or log in.** Sign up with the required details, log in, or request a password reset. Browsing does not require an account, and the decision on where login is required (add to cart, or checkout) is open (see section 9).
3. **Browse.** Open Shop All or a category, search, and filter. Each product card shows a real photograph, name and a starting price from the API.
4. **Open a product.** The detail page shows the gallery, description, price and options.
5. **Configure.**
   - Option groups are shown only if the API returns them for this product.
   - Required choices are marked; optional choices are labelled as optional.
   - Fabric uses swatches; size, closure and strap use chips; zipper and button colour use colour dots; lining and bow use toggles.
   - When a choice changes, the preview and the price breakdown update. The price shown is the server's quote.
   - If a choice is unavailable or invalid, it is disabled with a short reason.
   - **Add to cart** is disabled until all required choices are made.
6. **Add to cart.** The configuration is preserved, a cart drawer opens, and the customer sees feedback that the item was added.
7. **Review the cart.** The customer can change quantity, remove an item, or reopen the configuration to edit it. If a price or availability changed since the item was added, the cart says so.
8. **Buy Now or Checkout.** The customer proceeds to checkout.
9. **Choose an address.** Pick a saved address or add a new one, with validation for the fields.
10. **Review the order.** The final summary shows items with their configurations, delivery address and the total returned by the server.
11. **Pay.** The customer is taken to the payment experience. A payment redirect is never treated as proof of success.
12. **See the outcome.** After the backend confirms the payment status, the customer sees one of pending, success, failed or cancelled, with a clear next step for each.
13. **Order confirmation.** A confirmation page shows the order number and summary.
14. **My Account.** The customer can see saved addresses, My Orders and individual order details with status.

## 6. MVP (release 1)

Release 1 lets a customer find a product, configure it, pay for it and track the order, end to end, on mobile and desktop.

**Included:** the must-have features in section 4, plus About and Contact if time allows.

**Launch catalogue:** to be decided. Nine product types appear in the references (scrunchie, bow, mini pouch, makeup pouch, lipstick holder, sunglasses pouch, laptop sleeve, cable holder, bottle holder). The launch set is an open decision.

**Done means:**

- A new customer can sign up, configure a product, add it to the cart, pay in test mode and see the order in My Orders.
- Every screen handles loading, empty, error and success states.
- Prices and payment status shown always come from the backend.
- The site works on common mobile screen sizes and is usable with a keyboard.

## 7. Success metrics

No targets have been set. The numbers should come from the client, so they are left blank here rather than invented.

| Metric | What it tells us | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completed orders | Whether customers can buy end to end | To be set by client |
| Share of visitors who configure a product | Whether the configurator draws people in | To be set by client |
| Cart-to-checkout and checkout-to-payment completion | Where customers drop off | To be set by client |
| Account signups | Interest in returning | To be set by client |
| Failed or abandoned payments | Payment friction | To be set by client |
| Support messages about orders | Whether the site replaces manual chat ordering | To be set by client |

**Launch quality checks:** the full journey passes in test mode, the site works on common phones, and there are no blocking accessibility issues.

## 8. Not building in version one

- An admin dashboard. The team creates product records, prices, categories, images, fabrics, sizes and add-ons manually in the database.
- A hardcoded product catalogue in the frontend. Products come from the backend API.
- Fake reviews, fake customers or invented business claims.
- Fake product photographs presented as real inventory.
- Gift hampers and matching-set builders (listed as later features unless the client asks otherwise).
- Features outside the agreed list in section 4 that are not confirmed for this release.

## 9. Open decisions

These must be settled before the remaining documents are written.

- [ ] **Client confirmation:** do the problem statement and target-user assumptions (sections 1 and 2) match how the client sees their customers and current ordering process?
- [ ] **Launch catalogue:** which of the nine products ship in release 1?
- [ ] **Scrunchie sizes:** regular and large, or mini, medium and large (6, 9 and 11 cm in the size guide)?
- [ ] **Fabric list:** what are the real fabrics? The brief lists floral, corduroy, leopard, pink and blue; the moodboards add check, linen, canvas, velvet, satin and cotton.
- [ ] **Login requirement:** can visitors add to cart without an account, or must they log in first?
- [ ] **Brand name:** decided, or use a placeholder?
- [ ] **Photography:** will the client supply real product photos, or does the first build use clearly marked placeholders?
- [ ] **Payments:** which payment gateway will be used?
- [ ] **Delivery:** which areas does the client deliver to, and are shipping charges part of the order total?
- [ ] **Deadline:** is there a client delivery date?
- [ ] **Success targets:** which numbers should be set in section 7?
